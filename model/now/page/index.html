Calls log

Counted: called by AP, or by at least two of NBC, CNN, Fox News, ABC and CBS. Not yet counted: seen from one outlet only, or from an outlet outside that list such as Decision Desk HQ; these do not move any numbers. Seen: when the call was first found here, Eastern Time, not the time it was made. Winner's forecast chance: the winner's win probability before any results.
  • Called races. A race counts once AP, or at least two of NBC, CNN, Fox News, ABC and CBS, have projected a winner. Calls were collected through web searches of those outlets' live coverage and results pages, so a call can show up here a while after it was made. Ratings such as "leans Democratic" or "too early to call" are not calls.
  • Races not yet called. These come from the forecast of 3 November: the same poll averages, priors and error structure. Seats not up, — Democratic, — Republican and — independent senators and — Democratic and — Republican governors, are added as before.
  • How calls move the open races. In the forecast every race shares a national polling error, races in a region share a regional error and races in a state share a state error. A call tells us something about those errors: when a race the forecast saw as close is called, simulations whose shared errors fit that result become more likely. Technically, — draws of the shared errors are weighted by the exact probability that every called race goes the way it was called, then — simulated elections are drawn from them. The effective number of distinct draws after weighting is —. Across those draws the national polling miss now averages — points toward Democrats; before any calls it was zero. Early in the night the races that get called are the ones already clear in the count, while a race that is close in the count but not called is not adjusted for that, so this shift can run ahead of the final picture or behind it.
  • House. The — districts that any forecaster rated below safe are modelled one by one and take calls like the statewide races. The other — Democratic and — Republican seats are counted for the party they are rated safe for. An upset in one of them would not show here.
  • What is not used. Vote counts, the share of the vote counted, exit polls and network ratings. A race that is close in the count but not called stays at its forecast distribution, adjusted only through the calls elsewhere.
  • Limits. Maine and Alaska count ranked-choice ballots, and close races can take days, so some races were still open at the last refresh. Calls are occasionally withdrawn; none of the calls used here had been withdrawn when they were checked.
  • Counting. By ballot label, as in the forecast. Sanders and King, and any newly elected independent, count as I. Democrats need a majority including Sanders and King for Senate control; Republicans keep control at a tie through Vice President Vance.
