Senate. Our odds sit in the middle of the published forecasts: a little below Silver Bulletin and Race to the WH, close to Kalshi, and above Decision Desk HQ and the last Dave Manuel number we could read. Our Democratic caucus count matches Decision Desk HQ's; Race to the WH and Sabato's final ratings have one seat more. Polls underestimated Republicans in 2016, 2020 and 2024. This model treats that record as a reason for wide uncertainty, but not as a direction, because the misses in the 2018 midterm and the 2025 governor races went the other way.

House. Our odds are below Kalshi and the last Silver Bulletin and Dave Manuel numbers we could read, and above Decision Desk HQ. Our expected seat count is below Decision Desk HQ's and below the number of seats Sabato's final ratings lean to Democrats, but above the seats Cook rates at least Lean D before its toss-ups are counted. Part of the gap: half of each district estimate comes from the Cook, Sabato and Inside Elections ratings, and Cook and Inside Elections still list many Republican-held seats as toss-ups; the generic ballot alone would add a few Democratic seats. The rest likely comes from inputs we do not model, such as fundraising, and from holding every seat that all three raters call safe fixed in place.

Governors. Few public forecasts publish governor totals. Our central estimate equals the number of governorships Sabato's final ratings lean to Democrats.

How the forecast works

  1. Polls. — Senate and governor polls from — pollsters, plus — generic ballot polls, with fieldwork ending by —. We kept one version of each poll: likely voters and the full field where a pollster published several, and the final ranked-choice round in Alaska. For — lopsided governor races we used PollCheck's published average instead of individual polls.
  2. Weight from historical correctness. For each pollster we measured its race-adjusted excess error: how much bigger its miss on the final margin was than the misses of other polls of the same race, using polls taken in the last — days of the 2014 to 2022 general elections (538's raw poll database). The record is shrunk toward zero by n / (n + —). With fewer than — past polls we used 538's 2024 error rating plus — points; a pollster with no record gets — points. Weight = exp(−— × excess error), kept between — and —. Silver Bulletin's review of the 2025 races moved State Navigate up and Beacon/Shaw down.
  3. Other weights. Recency halves a poll's weight every — days. Sample size counts as the square root of n / —, capped at — respondents. Registered-voter polls get — of the weight and adult samples —. A pollster's repeated polls of one race share their weight.
  4. Likely voters. Registered-voter margins move by — points, the average gap between the likely- and registered-voter versions of the same survey across — pairs in this year's polls.
  5. Sponsored polls. Polls paid for by a campaign, party or allied group get — of the weight and are moved against the sponsor: — points for Democratic or independent sponsors and — for Republican ones. Those are the leans we measured for sponsored polls against nonpartisan polls of the same races, 1998 to 2022.
  6. House effects. Each pollster's average gap from the race averages, pooled across all 2026 races and the generic ballot and shrunk by n / (n + —), is taken off its polls. We repeat this — times.
  7. Historical state error. Final poll averages understate the eventual margin: results ran — times the poll margin in 2016 to 2022, so averages are stretched by that factor. States whose polls missed in the same direction across cycles get — of their average miss, capped at — point. We measured the persistence of those misses at a correlation of —, so most of the history is discounted. State-level 2024 misses are not in the data we could use.
  8. Prior for thin polling. Each average is blended with a prior. Senate: — fundamentals (generic ballot plus twice the state PVI plus incumbency) and — the Cook, Sabato and Inside Elections ratings. Governors: — fundamentals and — ratings, because governor races follow party lines less. Independent challengers get the ratings only. The prior counts as — unit of poll weight in the Senate and — for governors, so it matters only where polling is thin. Poll volume for this blend decays with a — day half-life.
  9. House. The — districts that any of Cook, Sabato, Inside Elections or the 270toWin consensus rate below safe are modelled one by one: — fundamentals on the 2026 maps and the rest ratings. The other — Democratic and — Republican seats are held fixed. Incumbency is worth — points in House and Senate races and — for governors.
  10. Correlated simulation. — simulated elections. Each race's margin is its estimate plus a national polling miss shared by every Senate, governor and House race (standard deviation — points), a regional error (—), a state error shared by races in one state (—) and a race error with fat tails (Student t, — degrees of freedom; — points in a well-polled statewide race, more where polling is thin, with independents, or with ranked-choice voting, and — in a House district). The national figure matches the measured spread of the average miss from cycle to cycle (—); competitive races missed by — points on top of that.
  11. No assumed direction. The national miss is centered on zero. Polls overstated Democrats in 2016, 2020 and 2024, but not in the 2018 midterm, and they understated Democrats in the 2025 governor races. The table below lists every cycle; the scenario chart on the overview shows what a repeat would do.
  12. Counting. By ballot label. Sanders and King (not up) and any newly elected independents count as I: Osborn, Achilles, Bengs or Bodnar in the Senate, Kiley in the House. Senate control needs — seats for Democrats including Sanders and King; Republicans keep control at a tie through Vice President Vance. The — Democratic, — Republican and — independent senators not up are added, as are the — Democratic and — Republican governors not up.

Assumptions and limits

  • Poll lists come from PollCheck's tables, which mirror Wikipedia. Some polls in some races may be missing.
  • Ratings include Sabato's final ratings, published the day before the election, and Cook's documented changes through the end of October. Inside Elections ratings are older: late October for the Senate, mid-October for the House and early September for governors, because its later changes were not public.
  • District PVIs come from the Wikipedia ratings table on the 2026 maps; Louisiana's 5th, redrawn this year, is estimated.
  • We do not know which caucus Osborn, Achilles, Bengs or Bodnar would join. If one of them decides the majority, the tile on the overview shows it as its own outcome.
  • No early-vote data, fundraising or candidate-quality model beyond what the ratings capture.
